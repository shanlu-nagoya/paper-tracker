## Abstract
This paper presents the design and full-wave electromagnetic analysis of a gold bowtie nano-optical antenna built for single-cell sensing and communication. A high-fidelity finite element model couples the antenna to a morphologically accurate biconcave erythrocyte. This model incorporates frequency-dependent optical properties for all biological components drawn from validated dispersion models. By analyzing how the cell's refractive index—which is tightly linked to hemoglobin concentration and overall physiological state alters the antenna's near-field distribution, a two-segment near-field channel model fitted to COMSOL data is established. Furthermore, an on-off keying (OOK) bit-error-rate analysis demonstrates the practical feasibility of optical nanocommunication using a single red blood cell. The dipole-like near-field radiation pattern confirms the spatial selectivity needed for precise single-cell interrogation. Ultimately, this framework establishes a solid foundation for advancing label-free biomedical diagnostics and enabling intracellular communication within the Internet of Nano-Bio Things.
