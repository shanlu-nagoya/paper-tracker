## Abstract
Free-space optical (FSO) communication can provide significantly higher transmission speed than traditional radio frequency (RF) communication and is expected to play an important role in the development of future 6G networks. In this paper, we propose and demonstrate an optical phased array (OPA) enabled space division multiplexed (SDM) coherent FSO system in which the OPA is implemented with 16 tunable phase shifters and 16 optical antennas on a photonic integrated chip. By employing a hill climbing algorithm to adaptively adjust the phase of the beams emitted from the 16 optical antennas, beamforming with precise direction control is enabled. Vertical and horizontal beam scanning is realized by wavelength and thermal tuning, respectively. To verify the SDM capability, four wavelengths with four beams pointing at four different directions are employed. Each wavelength is modulated with a 20 GBaud 16QAM signal. The system achieves an aggregate data rate of 320 Gb/s (4 × 80 Gb/s), delivers a minimum error vector magnitude (EVM) of 9.8% at a received optical power (ROP) of 0 dBm. Thanks to the adaptive equalizer based on the least mean square (LMS) algorithm, the power penalty is reduced by approximately 1.8 dB. The multi-beam FSO SDM system demonstrated in this study lays a strong foundation for future 6G networks, enabling higher transmission capacity and efficient parallel connectivity for multiple users.
