## Abstract
In Underwater Optical Wireless Networks (UOWNs), dynamic variations in particulate matter cause scattering. This scattering leads to signal attenuation in Underwater Optical Wireless Communication (UOWC) that is difficult to quantify and avoid, significantly limiting its development. Previous efforts often failed to accurately model the optical signal coverage area, overlooking the unintended interference (self-interference) within networks. This problem is especially severe in turbid environments, where Mie scattering dominates and beam spreading increases signal overlap between nodes. In this paper, we introduce the Maximum Error-Free Throughput (MEFT) problem to mitigate self-interference and consequently enhance network performance. To address this issue, we make the first attempt to propose a novel scattering signal coverage model that incorporates the directional bias of Mie scattering, thereby enhancing the accuracy of optical signal propagation descriptions. By discretizing the signal coverage area, the MEFT problem is solved by our dynamic transmission angle adaptation scheme named Optimal Transmission Angle Selection (OTAS). This scheme effectively minimizes signal overlap and interference by adjusting network topology and scheduling. Our experimental validations confirm the model’s precision, and both simulation and test-bed experiments demonstrate significant improvements in network performance compared to other baseline algorithms, achieving a 50.89% increase in error-free throughput.
